How do you fix wrong information about your business in AI answers?

A wrong fact in an AI answer is a quotation, and the way to correct a quotation is to correct what it quotes. The old address, the retired service, the price from three years ago: the engine took each one from a source it found, a listing, a directory, a review, an old page of yours, and it will keep taking it from there until the sources change. Owners write to the engine companies asking for a correction desk that does not exist. This guide gives the sequence that works: record the error exactly, trace it to its sources, fix the fact at every source, state the true fact quotably, and re-test until the answer moves.

Where does the wrong fact come from?

Almost always from the public record, in one of four places. Your own site, where a stale page, an old PDF, or a forgotten location page still states the fact. Your profiles and listings, where the fact was entered once and never revisited. Third-party pages, directories, articles, and reviews that repeat the fact and cannot be edited by you. And the model's own memory, where a fact learned in training is repeated without any live source at all. The first three are retrieval errors, fixed at the source. The fourth is slower, and the honest guide says so: a fact held in model memory changes when the corrected record is consistent enough, in enough places, for long enough that the next training run learns the new version. Retrieval is where to start, because most wrong facts in answers with citations are retrieval, and the citation names the source for you.

Step one: record the error exactly

Write down the question you asked, in the words you asked it, the engine, the date, and the wrong claim as stated. Screenshot it. An error that cannot be reproduced cannot be verified as fixed, and answers vary between runs, so the record is what separates a correction from a hope. The measurement guide sets out the observation discipline in full. Do this for every engine that serves your buyers, because the four engines draw on different sources and the wrong address in ChatGPT may be a right address in Perplexity, with different sources behind each.

Step two: fix the fact at every source you control

Your website first: the true fact stated plainly on the page a buyer would land on, in the form the question is asked, with schema that agrees with the visible text, per the schema guide. Then your profiles and listings, the same fact, the same wording, everywhere the business is described. Then the sources you do not control: the directory that takes corrections, the article's author, the platform's claim-and-correct process. Work the list in the order the answers cite them. A citation shown in the answer is the engine telling you which source it believed; fix that one first.

Step three: outvote the sources you cannot fix

Some sources never change: the archived article, the dead directory, the review from 2019. Corroboration is a vote, and a fact stated consistently by your site, your profiles, and your current listings outweighs a stale source the way three witnesses outweigh one. The entity work in the entity SEO guide is the standing version of this step: one set of facts, stated identically in every place the engines read, until the wrong version is the minority report in its own record. What does not work is stating the true fact in one place and leaving five stale statements standing. The engine is not choosing which version it likes. It is reporting which version it could verify most often.

How long until the answer changes?

Retrieved facts move at crawl speed: days to a few weeks after the sources are fixed and re-crawled, varying by engine and by how often the source is visited. Perplexity and Google's AI answers, built close to live indexes, usually move first. Facts an engine repeats from model memory move at retraining speed, which is measured in months and is not schedulable by anyone outside the company that trains the model. The practical discipline is the re-test: the same questions, the same engines, on a monthly cadence, with the answers recorded against the original error, until the correction holds across runs. That cadence is what the AI Presence, Measured service runs for clients who would rather read the report than run the questions.

Frequently asked questions

Can I contact ChatGPT or Google and ask them to correct an answer?

There is no correction desk that edits answers about a business, and feedback buttons on an answer report the response without changing your record. The answers are assembled from sources, so the correction has to happen at the sources: your site, your profiles, your listings, and the pages the answer cites. Fix those, and the answer follows at crawl speed.

Why does the AI answer still show my old address?

Because the old address is still stated somewhere the engine reads: a stale listing, a directory that scraped it years ago, an old page on your own site, or a profile you forgot existed. Search your business name with the old address and work through what you find. The version stated in the most places wins the answer, so every surviving copy has to be corrected or outvoted.

How do I stop wrong facts from coming back?

Keep one set of facts and maintain it as one document: when an address, a price, or a service changes, the change is made on the site, in the schema, in the llms.txt key facts, and on every profile in the same pass. Most wrong facts in AI answers are old truths that were corrected in one place and left standing in four others.
