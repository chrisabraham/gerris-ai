How do you write content AI search engines can cite?

A citable page gives a retrieval system something specific and trustworthy to use. It answers a real question, shows where its claims came from, and remains intelligible outside the page's surrounding marketing copy. No writing format guarantees an AI citation.

Published November 25, 2026. Written by Chris Abraham, Gerris.

Start with a real buyer question

Interview sales staff, review support questions, and examine search-query data. Distinguish informational curiosity from buying decisions. A question such as 'Can this pump tolerate continuous duty?' needs specifications and conditions, not a generic paragraph about innovation.

Answer before you advertise

State the answer in the first relevant paragraph. Then explain exceptions, evidence, and tradeoffs. Avoid introductory paragraphs that promise an answer without delivering one. A reader and a retrieval system should be able to identify the core claim without reading the whole page.

Publish facts that competitors cannot simply rewrite

Original tests, first-hand measurements, documented projects, product specifications, clear methodology, and named expert analysis provide distinctive value. Cite primary documentation for external claims. Do not invent customer outcomes, quotes, statistics, or certifications to make a page appear authoritative.

Use headings as a map of the argument

Organize each page around a coherent subject with descriptive headings and concise sections. Include comparisons where they help decisions, but make the criteria explicit. Tables are useful for specifications and tradeoffs; they are not a substitute for explanatory prose or accessible markup.

Separate evidence from interpretation

Label what was measured, when it was measured, and what conclusion is being drawn. If a test involved five products, do not imply that it establishes a universal ranking. If a claim is based on a vendor document, identify that source. Qualifying uncertainty makes the page more credible, not less.

Explain who wrote and reviewed the material

Identify the responsible author and relevant expertise, especially for technical or consequential advice. Maintain a meaningful update date when content actually changes. An author biography is a transparency feature, not a magic authority score.

Keep content extractable and accessible

Put important answers in crawlable text, not only in screenshots or embedded video. Use semantic HTML, descriptive links, alt text for informative images, and captions or transcripts where relevant. Avoid duplicating the same answer across dozens of thin pages.

What about FAQs and schema?

Write FAQs when the questions are genuinely useful. FAQ structured data should match visible content and comply with platform rules. Most sites should not expect Google FAQ rich results merely from adding markup, and no FAQ markup forces an AI assistant to quote the page.

How should content be maintained?

Assign owners to high-value pages. Recheck prices, availability, legal claims, standards, and product specifications on a schedule appropriate to the subject. Preserve stable URLs where possible, document material changes, and correct factual errors promptly.

How do you test whether writing helped?

Measure indexed accessibility, relevant search impressions, observed citations, qualified visits, and inquiries. Review which precise passages were used when citations are available. Do not infer causation from one AI answer changing after a rewrite.
