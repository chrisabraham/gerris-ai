What is entity SEO, and why does it matter for AI search?

Entity SEO is the practice of making it easy for search systems to recognize which real-world organization, person, product, or place a page describes. It is identity work: names, facts, and relationships stated consistently enough that the machines resolve every mention to one business, and the right one.

What is an entity?

An entity is a distinguishable thing: a company, a person, a product, a location. Two companies may share a name and remain different entities. One company may carry a legal name, a trading name, a founder's name, and three locations, and remain one entity with several descriptions. Search systems store records per entity, and every page, listing, and review is evidence filed into some entity's record. The failures are filing failures. Your reviews land in a namesake's record. A directory's stale address becomes your address. A merged record recommends a business that is half you and half a firm in another state, and buyers are quoted its prices as yours.

What facts form the canonical identity?

One set of facts, stated identically everywhere: the official name and known trading names, the domain, the physical address or the service area, the phone and email, the leadership, the services and products, and the registrations and licenses the business trades on. Publish that set on the website's canonical pages and repeat it, character for character where the field is structured, on every profile the business controls. A business without a public storefront states its service area honestly; it does not invent an address to satisfy a template, because an invented address is a false fact filed in its own record, where it will be corroborated against nothing and believed anyway.

How do external sources support recognition?

Independent directories, professional associations, government registries, legitimate news coverage, and verified platform profiles corroborate the identity from outside the business's own site. Their value is relevance and reliability, not raw count: one registry entry and one association listing outweigh forty profiles on directories nobody reads. Correct outdated records before creating new ones, because a new correct profile beside an old wrong one gives the machines a disagreement to resolve, and disagreements resolve toward whichever version is repeated more, not whichever version is true. Fake profiles created to multiply mentions are false filings, and they pollute the record they were meant to strengthen.

How should Organization and Person schema be used?

Describe the organization and its people with valid schema.org properties and stable @id values, so the website, the founder, the services, and the locations are declared as one connected record. Keep the JSON-LD aligned with the visible content: every name, price, and credential in the markup appears in the text a visitor can read. Structured data helps machines parse declared facts. It makes no claim true, and markup that declares facts the page does not state is treated as a false filing, as the schema guide sets out.

What does sameAs mean?

sameAs declares that a URL identifies the same entity: the organization's official profile on a platform, its listing in a registry, its page in an association directory. A customer's website is not the same entity. A sister company is not the same entity. An article about the business is not the same entity. Used correctly, sameAs is the strongest disambiguation signal a business can publish, because it hands the machines the list of profiles to merge. Used loosely, it instructs the machines to merge records that should stay separate, manufacturing the exact collision the work exists to prevent.

How do you handle name collisions?

Search your company name alongside its industry and its city, and inspect every competing entity the results surface. Then add the distinguishing facts, naturally and everywhere: the specialization, the city and service area, the founder's name, the founding year, the official domain. Where the business has prior names or acquisitions, document them plainly on the about page instead of erasing them; a documented history lets the machines file the old mentions into the current record, while an erased one leaves the old entity standing as a competitor you appear to be imitating. The collision case that recurs in audits is two same-name firms in different states whose reviews, hours, and prices have been merged into one record that recommends neither cleanly. The repair is the distinguishing record, stated in every place both businesses are described, until the merged file splits.

How does local entity work differ?

For local businesses the Google Business Profile is a primary source the answers quote directly, so its eligibility, primary category, hours, address or service area, and services get the same canonical discipline as the website. Directory information is kept consistent within each platform's rules. A service-area business does not fabricate a storefront. A multi-location brand distinguishes its real locations, each with its own record, from pages that merely name cities it would like to work in. Reviews belong to this layer too: they attach to the entity, and the themes reviewers repeat become the answer's description of the business, as the reviews guide covers.

Can you force a Knowledge Panel or an AI mention?

No. Google decides which entities receive a Knowledge Panel, and the answer engines decide which businesses their answers name. Neither is a deliverable anyone can sell. What the work controls is the accuracy and consistency of the sources those decisions are made from. A business with a settled record is eligible everywhere decisions are made from records. A business with a blurred record is eligible nowhere, and no panel or mention process exists that routes around the record.

How do you audit an entity?

Build the source inventory first: every page, profile, listing, and registry entry that describes the business, with its URL, the name it claims, the category, the address or service area, the contact details, and the date it was last verified. Flag contradictions by severity, wrong facts that reach buyers first. Fix the official sources, then the high-trust third-party records, filing corrections through each platform's process and documenting the requests that remain pending. The audit closes with a retest: the same name searches and buyer questions, checked against the inventory, per the audit checklist.

What does success look like?

Fewer incorrect facts in the answers. Fewer merged identities. Service descriptions that match across the website, the profile, and the listings. Discovery on the relevant queries, and citations that point at the right entity's pages. Success is tracked in observations of what the engines actually say, recorded over time, and never declared because a schema validator passed. Validation proves the markup parses. Observation proves the record settled.

Frequently asked questions

What is an entity in search?

A distinguishable real-world thing: a business, a person, a product, a place. Search systems keep a record per entity and file every page, listing, and review into the record it appears to describe. Entity SEO is the work of making sure your evidence is filed into your record, under your facts, and not into a namesake's.

How do I stop my business being merged with another company?

State the distinguishing facts everywhere both businesses are described: specialization, city and service area, founder, founding year, official domain. Publish Organization schema with a stable @id and sameAs links limited to your own official profiles. Then work the third-party records that carry the merged facts, in the order buyers encounter them.

Do I need a Wikipedia page for entity SEO?

No. Wikipedia is one corroborating source among many, and it is not a deliverable anyone can sell you, since its editors decide what qualifies. Registries, association listings, trade coverage, platform profiles, and a consistent canonical record on your own site corroborate an entity without it.

What does sameAs actually do?

It declares that the linked URL identifies the same entity as the page declaring it: your official profile, your registry entry, your association listing. It is the instruction machines use to merge profiles into one record, which is why it belongs only on URLs that are genuinely the same business, never on customers, sister companies, or articles about you.
