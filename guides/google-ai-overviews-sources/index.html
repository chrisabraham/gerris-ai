How do Google AI Overviews and AI Mode pick their sources?

Google AI Overviews and AI Mode pick their sources from the Google Search index, under the same eligibility rules as Search. Google states that there are no additional requirements to appear in either feature, and no special optimizations necessary: a page has to be indexed and eligible to show in Search with a snippet. The selection is where the features differ from the old results page. Both may turn one question into many related searches, a technique Google calls query fan-out, and link the pages that answer the pieces.

What is the difference between AI Overviews and AI Mode?

Google describes AI Overviews as a quick overview of a complicated topic with links for further reading, shown only when its systems judge that the overview adds value beyond the classic results. AI Mode is for questions that need further exploration, reasoning, or comparison, and returns a fuller AI-powered response with links to supporting sites. Google says the two may use different models and techniques, so the same question can produce different links in each.

For a business, the practical difference is depth. An AI Overview names a few sources at the top of an ordinary results page. AI Mode holds a conversation, and a buyer comparing vendors can ask follow-up after follow-up, each one a fresh chance to be named or left out.

What is query fan-out, and why does it matter?

Fan-out is how the features build an answer. Google says AI Overviews and AI Mode may issue multiple related searches across subtopics and data sources, and that while the response is being generated, the systems identify more supporting pages, which lets Google show a wider and more diverse set of links than a classic search.

The consequence is that a page is cited for the subquestion it answers, which is often narrower than the question the buyer typed. A roofing contractor's page that states what a permit requires in its county can be the linked source inside an answer about replacing a roof. Bing reports the same pattern in Microsoft's own numbers as grounding queries, which the Bing AI Performance guide explains. Pages that answer the follow-up questions directly, each under its own heading, give fan-out something to find.

What does Google tell site owners to do?

Google calls these fundamentals, worth doing and no different from good Search practice:

  • Ensuring that crawling is allowed in robots.txt, and by any CDN or hosting infrastructure.
  • Making content easily findable through internal links on the website.
  • Providing a great page experience for users.
  • Making sure that important content is available in textual form.
  • Supporting textual content with high-quality images and videos, when applicable.
  • Making sure structured data matches the visible text on the page.
  • Checking that Merchant Center and Business Profile information is up-to-date.

Each line maps to a guide here. The crawl gates are in the AI crawlers guide, the markup in the schema guide, and the profile in the Google Business Profile guide. The line that costs businesses most is the fourth: facts locked in images, scripts, and PDFs are facts the features read poorly or never.

How do you control what the features show?

With the controls that already govern Search. Google lists nosnippet, data-nosnippet, max-snippet, and noindex as the ways to limit what is shown from a page, and Googlebot's robots.txt rules govern crawl access. Google-Extended, the token many site owners reach for first, governs whether crawled content is used for Google's Gemini models and their grounding in its other AI systems, and Google has said it does not affect inclusion or ranking in Search.

Every one of these controls cuts both ways. A page marked nosnippet cannot be quoted in an AI Overview, and a page that cannot be quoted cannot be the source of the sentence that names your business. A business that wants to be named leaves its snippets on and its text open.

Where does AI Overviews traffic show up?

Inside the ordinary numbers. Google says sites appearing in AI features are included in the overall search traffic in Search Console, in the Performance report under the Web search type, with no separate filter for AI Overviews or AI Mode. In Google Analytics 4, the clicks land in Organic Search, and the AI Assistant channel GA4 added for chatbots excludes Google's own AI features, as the AI referral traffic guide covers.

Google says clicks from results pages with AI Overviews are higher quality, and points site owners to conversions and time on site as the measure. The honest measure of presence is older and simpler: ask the money questions in AI Overviews and AI Mode, in the buyer's words, on a schedule, and write down who was named. That record is what the monthly measurement keeps.

How do you check what AI Overviews and AI Mode say about you?

By asking, the same way every month, and writing the answers down:

  • List the money questions: twenty questions a buyer asks before hiring, in the buyer's words, with the city where the business works.
  • Ask both features: run each question in Google Search, read the AI Overview when one appears, then run it in AI Mode, from the same location each time.
  • Record three things: whether the business is named, how it is described, and which pages the answer links, yours and everyone else's.
  • Compare the linked pages: read the pages the answer chose over yours, and note what they state plainly that your page leaves implied.
  • Repeat on a schedule: the same questions, the same location, the same record, monthly.

Then read the record beside the Search Console numbers for the same questions, using the regex filters the Search Console guide sets out. The record says whether you were named; Search Console says what being named produced.

Why is a page that ranks well missing from the AI Overview?

Because ranking and citation are decided at different grains. Ranking scores a page against a query. The AI features compose an answer from passages, across the many searches fan-out runs, and link the pages whose passages carried the sentence. A page can rank third for the head term and hold no passage that answers any of the pieces, while a smaller site with one plain, direct paragraph on the right subquestion gets the link.

The fixes are textual. State the answer at the top of the section that answers it, under a heading phrased the way buyers ask, in words a model can lift without rewriting. Then make the same facts agree across the website, the Business Profile, and the listings, because the answer repeats the version of a fact the sources corroborate.

Frequently asked questions

Do I need special markup to appear in AI Overviews?

No. Google says there are no additional requirements to appear in AI Overviews or AI Mode, and no special optimizations necessary. A page has to be indexed and eligible to show in Search with a snippet. Structured data helps when it matches the visible text.

Can I keep my pages out of AI Overviews?

The controls Google documents for its AI features are the Search snippet controls: nosnippet, data-nosnippet, max-snippet, and noindex. They limit what is shown from a page in Search results as well, so using them trades away visibility in both places.

Does Search Console report AI Overviews separately?

No. Google includes traffic from AI features in the overall search traffic in the Performance report, under the Web search type, with no separate AI Overviews or AI Mode view.

Does blocking Google-Extended remove a site from AI Overviews?

No. Google-Extended governs whether crawled content is used for Google's Gemini models and their grounding, and Google says it does not affect inclusion or ranking in Search. AI Overviews and AI Mode follow Googlebot's rules and the snippet controls.
