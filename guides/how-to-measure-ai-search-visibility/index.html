How do you measure AI search visibility?

AI search visibility is not one number. A business can be mentioned without being recommended, cited without being praised, or visited without a referrer. Useful measurement separates those events and records exactly how the observations were collected.

Published November 25, 2026. Written by Chris Abraham, Gerris.

What should the measurement unit be?

Use a defined prompt-and-engine observation: the exact question, engine, date, region, search mode, response, and any cited sources. A prompt portfolio should represent real buying situations rather than hundreds of artificial variations of the brand name. Record the inclusion criteria before measuring.

What is mention rate?

Mention rate equals observations containing the target business divided by eligible observations, multiplied by 100. For example, 12 mentions in 40 observations is 30 percent. Keep the denominator visible, and separate branded questions from unbranded discovery questions. A brand mention in a question that already names the brand is not evidence of competitive discovery.

What is recommendation rate?

A recommendation is an answer that presents the business as a viable choice for the buyer's request. A neutral passing mention is different. Define the coding rule before scoring, document ambiguous cases, and preserve response excerpts for audit. You may also track whether the business is first, one of several, or explicitly ruled out.

How do citations differ from mentions?

A citation is a source reference or link used by an AI answer. It may point to your site, a directory, or a competitor. Count the cited domain and exact URL separately from whether the company is named in the prose. A citation does not prove that the answer endorsed the cited business, nor does a mention prove that the company's own site was retrieved.

How do you calculate competitive share?

For a fixed set of unbranded commercial prompts, count how often each eligible competitor is recommended. Because an answer may recommend several businesses, recommendation shares need not sum to 100 percent. State whether the metric is percentage of prompts, percentage of all recommendation slots, or percentage of named companies. Never silently switch definitions between reports.

How many tests are enough?

There is no universal minimum. Start with a manageable set of high-value buyer questions, stratified by intent, geography, and service. Repeat samples to estimate variability and retain the raw outputs. Treat small changes cautiously, especially when the underlying prompts or product versions change. More prompts do not repair a biased sample.

Can analytics show AI-driven conversions?

Review referral sources and landing pages in analytics, but recognize that some AI-driven visits arrive without identifiable referral information. Use tagged links only where you control the links, collect optional self-reported discovery sources, and compare inquiry quality over time. Do not label every direct visit as AI traffic.

What belongs in a monthly report?

Include the fixed prompt portfolio, methodology changes, mentions, recommendations, citations by domain, competitors, errors in business facts, landing-page visits where measurable, and actions completed. Show counts alongside percentages and provide the source observations. Separate measured results from hypotheses about why they changed.

What is a defensible success criterion?

Agree on business outcomes and intermediate signals before implementation: fewer factual errors, more qualified unbranded recommendations, more relevant citations, and better leads where attribution permits. A rise in mention rate is useful evidence, but it is not proof of incremental revenue or causation.
