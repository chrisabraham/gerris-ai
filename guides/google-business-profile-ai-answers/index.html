How does your Google Business Profile shape AI answers?

For a local or service business, the Google Business Profile is one of the primary sources an AI answer is built from. Its name, category, hours, service area, phone number, website link, and reviews are structured facts Google holds about the business, and Google lists checking that Business Profile information is up-to-date among its fundamentals for AI Overviews and AI Mode. A profile that agrees with the website corroborates it. A profile that disagrees, or a profile that is suspended or duplicated, corrupts the record at one of its sources.

Which profile fields feed an AI answer?

Every field that states a fact a buyer asks about:

  • Business name: the name the answer prints, and the key every other source is matched against.
  • Primary category: the plainest statement of what the business is, and the field most often chosen years ago and never revisited.
  • Address or service area: where the business is and where it works, which decides whether it fits a question that names a place.
  • Hours, including special hours: the field behind every question about who is open now or on a holiday.
  • Phone number and website: the facts the engines cross-check against the site itself.
  • Services and description: the services, in the profile's own words, which have to match the services on the website.
  • Reviews: what customers say about the work, in their words, with the business's replies.

Google's own AI features read the profile directly. Every other engine meets the same facts on the website and the listings that repeat them, so a wrong field on the profile tends to spread to the places that copy it.

How does Google decide which local businesses to show?

Google names three factors. Relevance is how well a Business Profile matches what someone is searching for. Distance is how far each business is from the person searching. Prominence is how well-known a business is. Google also says businesses with complete and accurate information are more likely to show up in local results, and that there is no way to request or pay for a better local ranking.

Those factors decide which businesses Google has in hand when it composes a local answer. Distance is fixed. Relevance and prominence are the profile's work: a precise category and a complete set of services make a profile relevant to more questions, and reviews, mentions, and a consistent record across the web make it prominent.

Which guidelines keep a profile standing?

Google's guidelines for representing a business are short, and breaking them invites suspension:

  • The real-world name: the name should reflect the business's real-world name, with no taglines, store codes, or extra keywords added.
  • One profile per business: Google says there should only be one.
  • A real location: the profile is created for the actual, real-world location, P.O. boxes are out, and a service-area business keeps one profile for its central office with a designated service area.
  • The fewest categories: choose the fewest categories it takes to describe the core business, and choose specific ones.
  • A phone the business controls: the number must be under the direct control of the business.

A suspended profile stops appearing in Google Maps and the local results, and the record loses its primary local source until the profile is reinstated.

What breaks the record most often?

From the audits, the same handful of failures:

  • A stale category: chosen for a service the business no longer leads with, so the profile describes a different company from the website.
  • A name stuffed with keywords: the city or the service added to the name, which reads as a win until the profile is suspended for it.
  • Duplicates after a move: the old location's profile still live, splitting reviews and facts across two records.
  • A phone number that differs from the site: often a tracking number on one source and the main line on the others.
  • Hours left unmaintained: holiday closures and changed hours that never reached the profile.
  • Verification stuck: a profile that was never verified, or lost verification, so the owner cannot correct it.

How do you audit a profile against the website?

Open the profile and the website side by side and check, field by field:

  • Name: identical on both, with nothing added on the profile.
  • Primary category: the plainest true name for what the business sells, matching the first sentence of the home page.
  • Phone: the same number on the profile, in the site's footer, and in the site's schema.
  • Hours: the same regular hours, and the next holiday's special hours already entered.
  • Website link: pointing at the right page, the location page for a multi-location business, and loading without a redirect chain.
  • Duplicates: a Maps search for the business's phone number and its old addresses, which surfaces any second profile still live.
  • Reviews: the date of the newest review and the share of the last twenty that have a reply.

Every mismatch goes on the fix list, with the website's version as the target unless the website is the one that is wrong.

How do you bring the profile and the website into line?

Start from the website, because it is the one source the business fully controls. Write the canonical statement there: the name, the category in the words buyers search, the services, the place, the phone number. Then set the profile to match it field by field, fix the listings to match both, and declare the connection in the site's LocalBusiness schema with a sameAs link to the profile, as the schema guide describes. Reply to reviews. Recheck the profile monthly, because edits suggested by the public and by Google can change fields without the owner asking.

Then ask the engines. Put the questions that bring you revenue to Google AI Overviews, AI Mode, ChatGPT, and Perplexity and record whether the answer now describes the business the way the profile and the website do. What about Bing and Apple?

The same work applies to the other maps. Microsoft says businesses can register with Bing Places for Business to help ensure that details such as address, hours, and contact information remain current and eligible for inclusion in AI-generated responses, and the Bing index feeds Copilot and the AI summaries in Bing. Apple Business Connect holds the business record for Apple Maps. Each is one more source the engines can check, and each has to say what the website says.

Frequently asked questions

Does ChatGPT use my Google Business Profile?

OpenAI does not publish a list of the local data sources ChatGPT uses. What every engine can do is check a business's facts across the sources it reads, and the profile's facts are repeated across maps, directories, and aggregator sites. A profile that matches the website strengthens the record everywhere it is copied.

Should my business name on the profile include keywords?

No. Google's guidelines say the name should reflect the business's real-world name, without taglines, store codes, or extra information. A keyword-stuffed name risks suspension, and a suspended profile drops out of the local results entirely.

Can I pay Google for a better local ranking?

No. Google says there is no way to request or pay for a better local ranking. Local results are decided by relevance, distance, and prominence, and complete, accurate profile information helps a business show up.

My profile was suspended. What now?

Find and fix the guideline problem first, then request reinstatement through Google Business Profile's own process. Until the profile is reinstated, the record AI answers draw on has lost one of its primary local sources, so the fix comes before any other AI search work.
