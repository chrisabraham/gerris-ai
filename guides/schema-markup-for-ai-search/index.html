What schema markup do AI answer engines actually use?

Schema markup is a statement of a page's facts in a form machines read directly, and answer engines use it to resolve, verify, and quote the businesses they name. A page of prose makes an engine infer: this string of words is a price, that one is a service area, this person runs the business. Schema removes the inference. In JSON-LD, the format Google recommends and this site uses, the page declares its facts as labeled data: the business's name, its category, its services, its prices, its people, each under its own field name. This guide covers the schema types that matter for AI search, what the markup actually does inside an answer, and the point where it stops working.

Which schema types matter for AI answers?

The working set is short, and every type on it states a fact buyers ask about:

  • Organization and LocalBusiness: the entity itself. Name, category, address, phone, service area, hours, and sameAs links to the business's profiles, which tell the engine that this website, this Google Business Profile, and this LinkedIn page are one entity, not three similar ones.
  • Service: each service the business sells, named in the category language buyers search, with the provider tied back to the entity.
  • Product and Offer: what is sold and at what price. A published price in schema is a fact an engine can quote with confidence, and prices are the facts buyers ask for most.
  • FAQPage: questions and answers in the buyers' own words, marked as questions and answers, ready to be lifted into a response.
  • Article: authorship, dates, and the publisher behind a piece of writing, which lets an engine attribute a claim to a named, accountable source.
  • Person: the people behind the business, their roles and credentials, tied to the organization they run.
  • BreadcrumbList and WebSite: the structure of the site itself, which pages sit under which, so a retrieved page arrives with its context attached.

Everything past this set is decoration for most businesses. A schema type that states no fact a buyer asks about earns nothing.

What does schema do inside an AI answer?

Recall the sequence from the guide on how AI answers choose businesses: retrieval, resolution, corroboration, composition. Schema works at the middle two stages. At resolution, entity schema with sameAs links settles which mentions belong to the same business, so a review, a listing, and a homepage assemble into one record instead of three partial ones. At corroboration, structured facts are cheap to compare across sources: a price stated in schema on your site and the same price stated on a directory listing corroborate each other in a way two paragraphs of marketing prose do not. The engine names and quotes the records it could settle. Schema is how a business files its facts in the form the settling is done in.

Where does schema stop working?

At the visible page. Schema that contradicts the text a visitor can read is discounted, and schema that invents facts the page does not state, services the business does not offer, reviews it does not have, prices it does not charge, is treated as a false filing, because that is what it is. The markup is a statement of the page's facts, not a second, better page hidden from humans. The desk's rule when writing it: every value in the JSON-LD must be findable in the visible text of the page it sits on. A business that follows that rule gets the full benefit of the format. A business that games it gets the markup ignored and, in Google's system, risks a structured data penalty on top.

How do you check the schema on your own site?

View the source of a money page and search it for application/ld+json; most business sites have either none or a plugin's generic minimum. Run the URL through Google's Rich Results Test and the Schema Markup Validator, which report errors and, more usefully, show you exactly which facts the machines extracted. Then read the extraction against the page. The test that matters is not whether the markup validates. It is whether the extracted record is the record you would state yourself: right category, right services, right prices, right people. The AI Visibility Audit includes that read, because schema errors are entity errors, and entity errors are what keep businesses out of answers.

One maintenance warning belongs in every schema conversation: the markup drifts. A redesign rebuilds the pages and drops the JSON-LD blocks. A plugin update replaces careful Service entries with a generic WebPage statement. A price changes in the visible text and stays old in the Offer. Each drift reopens the inference the markup was written to close, and it reopens silently, because the page looks identical to a human visitor. The check above takes minutes per page and belongs on the schedule after every site change, the same discipline the llms.txt guide prescribes for the machine layer as a whole: one set of facts, stated the same way, in every format the machines read.

Frequently asked questions

Does schema markup guarantee my business gets cited in AI answers?

No. Schema makes your facts easier to resolve, verify, and quote, and engines prefer sources that cost them the least doubt. It does not compel a citation. The businesses that get cited combine settled schema with consistent facts across their profiles and pages that answer buyer questions in quotable form. Schema is the filing system, not the whole record.

Should schema be written in JSON-LD or microdata?

JSON-LD. It is the format Google recommends, it sits in one block that can be audited at a glance, and it can be updated without touching the visible markup of the page. Microdata and RDFa scatter the same statements through the HTML, where they drift out of step with the text every time the page is edited.

Can schema markup hurt my site?

Yes, when it lies. Markup that states facts the visible page does not, fabricated reviews, invented prices, services the business does not offer, is treated as a false statement: engines discount it, and Google can apply a manual action against the site's structured data. Schema written strictly from the visible page carries no such risk.

What schema does a small business site need first?

Organization or LocalBusiness with sameAs links, one Service entry per service sold, FAQPage on pages that answer buyer questions, and Article on anything published under a byline. That set states who you are, what you sell, and what buyers ask, which is the record the answer engines assemble.
