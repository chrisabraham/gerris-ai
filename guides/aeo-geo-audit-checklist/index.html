What should a professional AEO and GEO audit include?

A useful AEO or GEO audit is not a PDF full of acronyms. It establishes how your business currently appears in AI-assisted discovery, identifies evidence-based causes of errors or omissions, and ranks fixes by impact and effort. Here is what to demand before paying.

Published November 25, 2026. Written by Chris Abraham, Gerris.

What questions should the audit answer?

Which buyer questions matter, where is the business mentioned or recommended, what sources are cited, what facts are wrong, which competitors appear instead, and which fixes are under your control? Require the scope and target markets in writing before testing begins.

How should the baseline be collected?

Use a documented prompt set covering branded, unbranded, category, comparison, problem, and local intent as applicable. Record exact prompts, engine and search mode, date, geography, answers, and cited URLs. Preserve raw observations, not just a colorful dashboard score.

What technical review is necessary?

Inspect crawl access, robots directives, canonicalization, indexability, redirects, sitemaps, HTML rendering, internal links, and relevant structured data. Identify concrete affected URLs and reproducible problems. Do not confuse a valid schema test with evidence of AI recommendation.

What should an entity review include?

Compare official site claims with business profiles, directories, association records, and credible external references. Identify conflicting names, categories, service areas, personnel, and contact information. Distinguish sources the business can edit from those requiring third-party corrections.

How should content and citation gaps be assessed?

Map high-value buyer questions to pages that answer them. Evaluate whether the pages provide direct answers, original proof, transparent authorship, and verifiable claims. Review competitor sources to identify missing evidence, not to recommend copying their wording.

How should recommendations be prioritized?

Each recommendation should state the observed issue, affected pages or profiles, expected business relevance, proposed change, estimated effort, owner, dependencies, and validation method. Separate quick corrections from content production, external outreach, and platform-dependent outcomes.

What deliverables should the buyer receive?

Ask for an executive summary, raw prompt observations, technical findings, source inventory, competitor comparison, prioritized implementation backlog, and retest protocol. A useful audit should remain actionable if you hire a different implementer.

What are the warning signs?

Avoid guaranteed ChatGPT placements, invented proprietary ranking factors, anonymous citations, mass-generated doorway pages, fake reviews, and unexplained scores. Beware vendors who insist every site needs the same plugin or llms.txt file without examining its actual problems.

How much should an audit cost?

Scope matters more than an arbitrary market average. How do you know whether implementation worked?

Retest the same prompt set under documented conditions, validate technical fixes independently, and monitor business outcomes where attribution is possible. Report unchanged or negative results as plainly as improvements. The deliverable is a defensible record, not a promise that an AI system will comply.
