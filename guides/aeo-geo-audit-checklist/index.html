What should a professional AEO and GEO audit include?

A useful AEO or GEO audit is not a PDF full of acronyms. It establishes how your business currently appears in AI-assisted discovery, identifies the evidence-based causes of errors and omissions, and ranks the fixes by impact and effort. This checklist is what to demand before paying anyone, including Gerris.

What questions should the audit answer?

Six, in writing, before testing begins. Which buyer questions matter? Where is the business mentioned or recommended, and where is it absent? What sources are the answers citing? What facts about the business are wrong? Which competitors appear instead, and on what evidence? Which fixes are under your control, and in what order should they be done? The scope, the target market, and the engines to be tested are agreed in writing as part of the same document, because an audit that discovers its scope halfway through has already spent your money finding out what it was for.

How should the baseline be collected?

With a documented prompt set covering branded, unbranded, category, comparison, problem, and local intent. Gerris runs 24 buyer questions, in the buyers' own words, across ChatGPT, Perplexity, Google AI Overviews and AI Mode, and Claude. For every observation the audit records the exact prompt, the engine and search mode, the date, the geography, the full answer, and the cited URLs. Raw observations are preserved and delivered, not summarized into a dashboard score. A score you cannot audit is a number you are asked to take on faith, and faith is not a deliverable.

What technical review is necessary?

Crawl access and robots directives, canonicalization, indexability, redirect chains, sitemaps, HTML rendering, internal links, and structured data, each finding tied to concrete affected URLs with a reproducible test. The review compares the answers' cited sources against the homepage and up to five money pages, because those are the pages the answers should be built from. A valid schema test is evidence that markup parses. It is not evidence of an AI recommendation, and an audit that presents it as such has confused the filing with the verdict.

What should an entity review include?

A comparison of the official site's claims against the business profiles, directories, association records, and credible external references, covering names, categories, service areas, personnel, and contact information. The review distinguishes sources the business can edit itself from those requiring third-party correction processes, and it checks name, address, and phone consistency across the site, the Google Business Profile, and the listings. When sources disagree, answers name the business whose facts line up; the entity review is the inventory of everywhere yours do not.

How should content and citation gaps be assessed?

Map the high-value buyer questions to the pages that answer them, and mark the questions with no page, the pages that answer a question nobody asks, and the pages whose answer is buried under marketing copy. Evaluate direct answers, original proof, transparent authorship, and verifiable claims. Review the sources competitors are cited from to identify missing evidence, the test your record fails that theirs passes. The output is a gap list, not a recommendation to copy a competitor's wording; their wording is their evidence, and copying it files their record, not yours.

How should recommendations be prioritized?

Each recommendation states the observed issue, the affected pages or profiles, the business relevance, the proposed change, the estimated effort, the owner, the dependencies, and the validation method that will prove the fix landed. Quick corrections are separated from content production, content production from external outreach, and all three from outcomes that depend on a platform's schedule. The order follows the way answers are assembled: wrong facts and blocked pages first, because no later improvement survives a record that contradicts itself at the source.

What deliverables should the buyer receive?

An executive summary that gives away the verdict on the first page. The findings in full, in prose, with the evidence attached. A playbook in which every job is stated as why, what, and how, in numbered steps an implementer can execute without a follow-up meeting. The raw prompt observations, the source inventory, and the competitor comparison as appendices. And a retest protocol that says exactly how the work will be verified. A useful audit remains actionable if you hire a different implementer, or none. An audit that only its author can act on is a sales document with page numbers.

What are the warning signs?

Guaranteed ChatGPT placements. Proprietary ranking factors the vendor will not explain. Citations quoted without the questions that produced them. Mass-generated doorway pages offered as a content strategy. Fake reviews, in any quantity, at any price. Unexplained scores. And the vendor who insists every site needs the same plugin or the same file before examining the site in front of them. Each of these is a tell that the deliverable is the sale, and the audit is its packaging.

How do you know whether implementation worked?

Retest the same prompt set under the same documented conditions. Validate the technical fixes independently, on the live site, at the URLs the audit named. Monitor business outcomes where attribution is possible, and report unchanged and negative results as plainly as improvements. The audit's final deliverable is a defensible record: what the answers said, what was changed, what the answers say now. Anything shorter is a promise wearing a report's clothes.
