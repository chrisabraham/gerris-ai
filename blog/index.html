Blog: field notes on AI search, written from the work

The Gerris blog carries the working notes behind the practice: how the answer engines read a business, how they summarize it, and what earns a name inside the answer. The guides to answer engine optimization and generative engine optimization state the method in full. The blog works the same ground one rule, one failure, and one fix at a time, at the length each subject needs, in the plain register the guides use.

How AI engines read your business: literal words, mandatory summaries, consistent facts

The three working rules behind every Gerris engagement, expanded: engines take your words at face value, the summary of your business gets written with or without you, and consistency across your site, profile, and listings decides whose facts the answer repeats.

What does the blog cover?

AI search as business owners meet it: the questions buyers ask Google AI Overviews, ChatGPT, Perplexity, and Gemini; the record those answers are assembled from; and the clerical work—canonical pages, entity schema, consistent listings, honest FAQ content—that decides which businesses the answer names. News in AI search runs in the AI Search Briefing; the blog is the slower file, where a rule gets stated once and kept.

Who writes the blog?

Chris Abraham, who leads Gerris. Websites since 1994, SEO since 1998, and the last several years spent on the answer engines that now sit on top of search. Every post states a working rule from live engagements and says what the rule means for a business that needs to be named when an AI assistant answers its buyers.
